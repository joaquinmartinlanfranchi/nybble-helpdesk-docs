Nybble AI Help Desk

An AI-assisted help desk: it answers first-line queries from an approved knowledge base, classifies and prioritizes, and escalates to a human agent when it can't resolve. This document reconstructs how the product works.

Status: Version 1 Backlog: Jira LABS

Product Vision 01

What it is, what problem it solves, for whom, and how far it reaches today.

The AI Help Desk is a multi-tenant help desk with an AI agent on the front line. The User converses over chat; the Bot / AI Agent looks for the answer in the knowledge base that the KB Manager loads and maintains, under the configuration set by the Tenant Admin. If it finds it, it answers and the case closes on its own; if not, it becomes an incident that enters a queue and is picked up by a human Agent / Operator.

The problem

First-line support saturated with repetitive queries and slow first response — right when the human team has the least capacity (go-live, hypercare).

The proposal

Deflect repetitive queries with AI scoped to approved knowledge, immediate 24/7 response, and reserve for the human what the AI can't resolve, with automatic classification.

Current functional scope

In scope (works today)
  • Chat with an AI bot scoped to the tenant/company knowledge base
  • Automatic classification of priority, category and module
  • Automatic escalation (3 failed attempts) and on user request
  • Escalation inbox with exclusive case-taking (first-click-wins)
  • Operator actions: Resolve, Reject, Return, Approve and create in Jira
  • Mandatory logging of resolution time
  • User history ("My Queries") with fixed status filters
  • KB uploads isolated by tenant and by company
  • Operator, tenant-admin and AI-observability metrics
  • Organization/tenant creation by the Tenant Admin
Out of scope for V1
  • Operator ↔ user chat within the platform. The conversation after escalation is handled via Slack or email, outside the desk LABS-516
  • SLA framework. "SLA timers (no SLA framework planned)" LABS-465
  • Image interpretation by the bot LABS-515
  • Email notifications for status changes (the preference exists but doesn't work; the panel was hidden)
  • Manual priority override by the operator when approving an incident (with delta logging to improve the classifier)

Users & Roles 02

Four human roles, a three-level hierarchy, and one non-human actor that decides.

The four roles

User The one with the problem
Who they are
The end user of the supported system (a Nybble employee using NYBO, or a client's user in an Odoo project). Demo account: user@acme.com.
Goal
Resolve their question or problem as fast as possible, regardless of whether it's the AI or a person who resolves it.
What they can do
  • Chat with the bot; attach files with the + button only after the bot's first response LABS-515
  • Explicitly ask to talk to a human (escalates immediately)
  • Confirm the creation of the incident when the bot proposes it
  • Answer "Did this answer help you?" with Yes / No
  • Close the conversation (red button or the X)
  • View their history in My Queries and filter by status
  • Check the FAQ
Agent / Operator The one who resolves what the AI couldn't
Who they are
The support human. Can be from Nybble (Innovation for NYBO, support lead for BS) or from the client itself if it hires the desk as a product. Demo account: agent@acme.com.
Goal
Clear the escalation queue: take each case, decide what applies, and log the time spent.
Available actions
  • Take case — exclusive assignment, first click wins LABS-479
  • Resolved — closes without an external ticket; adds 1 to "resolved by me"
  • Reject — cases that don't apply (hallucinations, unrelated topics)
  • Return — opens a text box to explain the reason and what the user should do (the only CTA with a text box)
  • Approve and create in Jira — closes the interaction on the desk and creates the external ticket
  • Resolution time — hours and minutes, a required field for any of the actions LABS-477
What they see
  • Inbox filtered by the clients assigned to them: clientId IN (assigned clients) — an assignment made by the Tenant Admin from Analyst Management
  • Detail with the user's literal query + AI summary + status, priority, agent, channel, client and contact
  • Escalations ordered CRITICAL > HIGH > MEDIUM > LOW
  • Only their own metrics in My Performance — BR-070
Critical restriction The operator cannot chat with the user within the platform. The Approve/Resolved/Reject buttons don't open a text box; only Return does, and without attachments. In practice the operator "resolves" by logging hours and marking the status. Communication happens over Slack or email, and the resolution time must be documented in order to change the incident's status.
KB Manager The owner of the knowledge that feeds the desk
Who they are
"the client's user who will administer the knowledge and documentation that feeds the desk." Demo account: kb@acme.com. Created by the Tenant Admin LABS-553.
Goal
Make the desk assertive. The quality of the desk is a direct function of the quality and coverage of the documentation loaded.
What they can do
  • Upload documents to the Knowledge Base
  • Scope each document to one or several companies within the tenant; if none is assigned, the document is tenant-wide LABS-517
  • View the indexing status per document: Indexing / Ready / Error LABS-488
  • View the topic summary by company
Future versions
  • KB scope fed by external sources (Google Drive) and per-company indexes LABS-576
Tenant Admin The owner of configuration and global metrics
Who they are
"the admin of each organization, I think mostly it would be us." Demo account: admin@acme.com.
Goal
Configure the desk's behavior for their organization and measure its performance and cost.
What they can do
  • View all global tenant metrics: tickets and times by client and by human agent LABS-484
  • Create KB users LABS-553
  • Create and manage Analysts (Agents/Operators) from Analyst Management, assigning each one one or several companies — that determines which escalations they see in their Inbox confirmed in the platform
  • Bot configuration: view and regenerate the KB's topic summary LABS-488
  • Adjust per-tenant parameters: maxAttempts (2–5, default 3), maxMessagesPerSession, confidence threshold, bot name and tone
  • Settings → AI Observability section: tokens, cost and quality score LABS-529
  • Organization creation ("New Organization" form): organization name, admin email (adminEmail) and password LABS-532
Bot / AI Agent A system actor, not a user

It appears in the sources as BOT, AI Agent, front-desk agent or help desk agent. Functionally it is a tool-use agent: it doesn't follow a fixed pipeline, it decides which tool to use on each turn LABS-472.

ToolWhat it does functionally
searchKnowledgeBaseSearches the tenant/company KB. It is the only valid source of data for answering.
createTicketDraftProposes a pre-categorized incident to the user. Requires explicit user confirmation.
escalateToAgentEscalates to the human queue, attaching a generated summary of the whole conversation.
checkTicketStatusAnswers the status of an existing incident.
rejectOffTopicRejects queries outside the tenant's domain. The orchestrator reinforces the rejection programmatically based on the KB search state.

Invariants the bot cannot violate: tenant isolation, no ticket creation without approval, and never making up answers. Two pre-LLM safety nets are also kept: detection of critical keywords (escalates immediately) and sentiment analysis.

It's a scoped RAG bot, not a general conversational LLM The KB's topic summary is injected into the prompt only to detect whether something is out of scope, and the instruction is literal: "NEVER answer questions using data from this summary — ALWAYS call searchKnowledgeBase". This was hardened after a real bug in which the bot answered with fragments of the summary without searching the KB LABS-501.

Core Concepts 03

The HelpDesk's vocabulary.

Tenant

The largest unit of isolation — typically a client organization. It has its own KB in a separate namespace, its own metrics, and its own bot configuration. A user belongs to a single tenant. Created by the Tenant Admin.

Client / Company / Organization

The three terms are used as synonyms. It's the intermediate layer within the tenant: it lets a tenant serve several clients without cross-contamination of tickets, conversations, or agent assignments. KB documents and agent assignments operate at this level.

User

Belongs to a tenant and a client, and has exactly one functional role. Only sees their own data.

Query

The full conversation the user starts with the bot. If resolved via the KB, it ends up Resolved by AI and never reaches a human.

Incident / Escalation

The escalated query. It enters a shared queue ("ticket pool") seen by all operators enabled for that client. It carries: the literal query, an AI-generated summary, priority, category, module, client, contact, channel, status, and resolution time when closed.

Jira Ticket

The handoff to an external system when the desk can't close the case. Created only if the operator approves. The reference (number) is saved for traceability, and the user gets a confirmation with that number (BR-033).

Knowledge Base (KB)

The set of approved documents that feeds the bot. It is the only valid source of answers. Isolated by tenant and optionally scoped to companies. Each document goes through indexing (PENDING → INDEXING → INDEXED / ERROR).

KB topic summary

A short, deliberately opaque summary (topic labels, no data or numbers) injected into the prompt only to detect off-topic queries. It's regenerated automatically on any change to the KB, and the admin can regenerate it manually.

Priority

CRITICAL > HIGH > MEDIUM > LOW. Assigned by the AI using a common rubric, and it orders the operator's inbox.

Resolution Time

A numeric field of hours and minutes that the operator fills in by hand and that is required for any of the closing actions. It's the input for every time KPI: there is no automatic measurement.

Conversation session

The chat context. It has a counter of failed KB attempts, a cap on messages per session, and it clears on inactivity. The 45- and 60-minute timers operate on it.

"Finalized" status

The logical deactivation of a client or a tenant: no more users or operations are created, the User role cannot log in, and the data remains read-only for the higher roles LABS-486.

Guided Tour 04

Log in with the four roles and walk through the product in this order.

Suggested tour to understand the product

Two browser sessions (or two profiles) in parallel: one with user@acme.com and another with agent@acme.com. The whole lifecycle is best understood by watching both sides at once.

Step 1 · The AI happy path user
  1. Log in as user@acme.com and open the chat.
  2. Ask a question that is covered in the KB (try something about vacation or leave policy: those are the examples used in the tests).
  3. Notice that the red finish button only appears after the bot's first response.
  4. Close the conversation and answer Yes to "Did this answer help you?".
  5. Go to My Queries: the query is now Resolved by AI and requires no action from you.
Step 2 · Escalation user
  1. Open a new chat and ask something the KB can't answer, or directly ask to talk to a human.
  2. Verify that when it escalates the chat closes on its own and does not ask whether it was helpful: the escalation is already a closure.
  3. Go to My Queries: the incident shows as Pending.
  4. Check what shows on the card: title, body and tags.
Step 3 · The operator's side agent
  1. Log in as agent@acme.com and open the Escalation Inbox.
  2. Look at the KPIs at the top and see how they relate to the incidents' statuses.
  3. Open the incident you created. It appears at the bottom of the list: if the screen looks static, scroll (known bug LABS-569).
  4. Check that you can see the user's literal query and the AI summary, plus priority, category, client and contact.
  5. Take the case and watch it move to In Review.
  6. Try the four CTAs on different incidents: Return (the only one with a text box), Resolved, Reject, Approve and create in Jira. All of them will require the resolution time.
Step 4 · Knowledge and isolation kb
  1. Log in as kb@acme.com and go to Knowledge Base.
  2. Upload a .doc or PDF document and scope it to the relevant company.
  3. Check the document's indexing status.
  4. Go back to the chat as the user and ask about that content: confirm that the answer respects the per-company scope. This is the test the team ran twice successfully.
Step 5 · Administration and measurement admin
  1. Log in as admin@acme.com.
  2. Global metrics: tickets and times by client and by human agent.
  3. Settings → AI Observability: tokens, cost, average score, KB matching efficiency, filters by date/model/feature. Read the KPI section before drawing conclusions: the average score does not cover the main chat.
  4. Look at the bot configuration and the KB topic summary with its Regenerate button.
Step 6 · The timers (if you're patient)
  • Leave a conversation without answering the helpfulness question: at 45 minutes the chat closes and it's assumed to have been helpful.
  • Leave a chat idle: at 60 minutes the history and context are cleared; if there was a KB match, it's taken as resolved by the bot.
  • Both behaviors were manually verified by QA on 08/13.
